LEADERSHIP DISCUSSION

GCGA & Orbit: production tradeoffs

Shared enterprise GPTs and a shared operational workspace serve different team needs.

Enterprise GPT experience described by the team versus Orbit’s proposed production role
Decision areaGCGA / enterprise GPTsOrbitTradeoff
Creation & sharingEmployees can create GPTs and submit a request to share them with specific organizations or across Oracle, subject to approval.Teams use shared or team-specific specialist agents within their operational workspace.Enterprise sharing supports broad reuse. Orbit adds team-specific operational context; enterprise sharing and onboarding requirements still apply where relevant.
Team visibilityUsers access shared GPTs through the chat UI. In the experience described, members work in separate conversations; sharing a GPT does not itself provide a common operational run view.Authorized team members share visibility into agent execution, approval status, workflow progress, and recorded outcomes.Individual chat offers flexible assistance. A shared workflow view improves coordination and handoffs, but requires clear access and approval permissions.
Specialist execution & hostingThe employee-facing GPT experience is accessed through chat; this comparison does not assess backend execution or hosting capabilities.Production specialist-agent services could run on OCI VMs or OKE clusters and connect to approved APIs and existing automation.Dedicated deployment supports operational integration and execution control. Orbit owners must manage credentials, resource isolation, reliability, and recovery.
Ownership & reuseUses the established enterprise distribution and governance process for shared GPTs.Coordinates operational workflows while reusing enterprise identity and each team’s existing automation where appropriate.Validate the added operational value before funding another service. Assign one execution owner per workflow to avoid duplicate changes.
